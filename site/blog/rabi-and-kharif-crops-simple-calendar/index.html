Rabi and Kharif Crops: A Simple Calendar for Indian Farmers

Indian farming runs on three seasons. Knowing them helps you understand why prices rise and fall through the year.

By MandiRates Editorial Team · Published 27 Nov 2026

The three seasons

  • Kharif: sown with the monsoon (about June to July) and harvested around September to November. Examples: paddy, maize, soybean, cotton, bajra, groundnut and arhar.
  • Rabi: sown after the monsoon (about October to December) and harvested around March to April. Examples: wheat, mustard, gram, barley and peas.
  • Zaid: grown in the short summer gap (about March to June). Examples: watermelon, cucumber and moong.

Why seasons matter for prices

When a crop is harvested, a lot of it reaches the mandis together. This is called heavy arrivals, and prices are often softer. Later in the year, when less is arriving, prices are often firmer.

This is a common pattern, not a rule. Weather, demand, imports and government policy can change it in any year.

How to use this on MandiRates

Open the crop page and look at the trend. Compare today’s price with its recent average. If you can store your produce safely, the trend can help you decide when to sell.

A quick checklist

  • Note which season your crop belongs to before you plan sales.
  • Watch the price trend in the weeks after harvest.
  • Keep storage ready if you plan to hold produce.
  • Compare more than one mandi before you load.

Common mistakes

  • Selling everything in the first week of heavy arrivals when you could store safely.
  • Assuming last year’s price pattern will repeat exactly.
  • Ignoring local sowing dates given by your agriculture office.

Frequently asked questions

What is the Zaid season?

The short summer season between Rabi and Kharif, roughly March to June.

Is wheat a Rabi or Kharif crop?

Wheat is a Rabi crop.
