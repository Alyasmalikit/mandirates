Why Mandi Prices Differ From Place to Place

The same crop can sell for very different prices in two mandis on the same day. Here is why.

By MandiRates Editorial Team · Published 27 Nov 2026

The main reasons

  • Local supply: a mandi with heavy arrivals often has lower prices.
  • Quality and variety: better grade and a preferred variety fetch more.
  • Demand nearby: mills, traders and exporters buy where demand is.
  • Distance: transport adds cost, so prices rise away from the growing area.
  • Market fees and commission: these differ by state and mandi.
  • Weather and festivals: rain, heat and festival demand can move prices quickly.

Compare fairly

Do not look at the headline price alone. Subtract transport, loading, commission and the cost of waiting. The Where to sell tool on MandiRates does the transport part for you.

Check the date

Always look at the date next to a price. A price from two days ago is not today’s price, and MandiRates shows the date for every row.

A quick checklist

  • Compare at least three mandis for your crop.
  • Check the date of each price.
  • Add transport and commission to each option.
  • Call the mandi to confirm the rate before you load.

Common mistakes

  • Choosing the mandi with the highest price without counting transport.
  • Trusting an old price.
  • Forgetting that quality and variety change the rate.

Frequently asked questions

Is the highest price always the best?

No. Transport, commission and payment time can make a lower price the better deal.
