Tomato: Handling Tips and Why Prices Move So Fast

Tomatoes spoil fast, so price and handling go together. A good price is easy to lose if the fruit is damaged on the way.

By MandiRates Editorial Team · Published 27 Nov 2026

Why tomato prices swing

Tomato is perishable and is grown in many regions across the year. A change in weather or a sudden jump in arrivals can move prices a lot in a few days.

Handling tips

  • Harvest at the right stage for the distance it has to travel.
  • Use crates, not heavy sacks, so the fruit is not crushed.
  • Keep the produce in shade and move it quickly.
  • Sort out damaged fruit so it does not spoil the rest.

Choosing where to sell

Compare nearby mandis on MandiRates and subtract transport. For a fast-spoiling crop, a nearer mandi with a slightly lower price can leave you with more money.

A quick checklist

  • Harvest at the right stage for the distance.
  • Use crates and keep the load in shade.
  • Choose the nearest good mandi.
  • Check the price just before you load.

Common mistakes

  • Packing in heavy sacks that crush the fruit.
  • Driving far for a small price gain.
  • Mixing damaged and good fruit in one lot.

Before you sell

This is general guidance. Sowing and harvest times differ by region and year, so check with your state agriculture department or your local Krishi Vigyan Kendra (KVK) before you decide.

Frequently asked questions

Why are tomato prices so unstable?

Tomatoes spoil fast and supply changes quickly with weather and region, so prices can swing a lot.
